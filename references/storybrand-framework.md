# The 7-part structure, adapted for a personal brand

StoryBrand (Donald Miller) uses the shape of a story to make a message clear. The core idea: people ignore what they cannot quickly connect to their own goals. A clear story makes the connection for them.

For a personal brand, the person reading your materials (usually a hiring manager or design/engineering/product leader) is the hero. You are the guide who helps them win.

| # | Element | Personal-brand question | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A character** | Who is reading, what do they want, and who do they want to become? | Making yourself the hero |
| 2 | **Has a problem** | What practical problem do they face (external)? How does it make them feel (internal)? Why shouldn't anyone have to accept it (philosophical)? What causes it (villain)? | Listing your skills instead of their problem |
| 3 | **Meets a guide** | How do you show you understand them (empathy)? What proves you can help (authority)? | A wall of achievements |
| 4 | **Who gives them a plan** | What 3 simple steps describe working with you? | Jargon-heavy methodology names |
| 5 | **And calls them to action** | What one clear thing should they do (direct)? What lower-commitment step builds trust (transitional)? | Passive CTAs like "download resume" |
| 6 | **That helps them avoid failure** | What realistically goes wrong if the problem stays unsolved? | Fear-mongering, or "hire me or fail" |
| 7 | **And ends in success** | What does their world look like when the problem is solved? | Describing your activities, not their result |

## Rules of thumb
- **Clarity beats completeness.** Cut anything that does not fit one of the 7 elements.
- **Failure is salt, success is flour.** A little failure, a lot of success.
- **Internal problems drive decisions.** People hire to solve how a problem makes them feel, not only the task.
- **A plan reduces risk.** Three steps make you feel easy to work with.
- **Repeat the same CTA wording everywhere.**
- **A tagline should state a result the audience gets,** not describe your method.
- **Aspirational identity:** name who the audience wants to become, and connect your help to it.
