# Stage guide

Work one stage at a time. Each needs approval before the next.

## Stage 1 · Character
- Inputs: target JDs, stated career direction.
- Deliver: primary audience (role, company type, need), secondary audiences, what they want, aspirational identity, a one-sentence Character statement.
- Check: is this the person who actually makes the hiring decision?

## Stage 2 · Problem
- External: the practical, organizational problem (from JD language).
- Internal: how it makes them feel. Mark as a hypothesis unless backed by conversations, interviews or insider chats.
- Philosophical: one "should not have to" sentence.
- Villain: a systemic force, not a person. Prove it is real with examples from the candidate's own projects.

## Stage 3 · Guide
- Empathy: a short, true statement showing they understand the audience. Lived experience beats claims.
- Authority: one smooth sentence, plus 3 to 5 proof points mapped to the brand pillars.
- Testimonials: map each recommendation to what it proves. Name what no testimonial covers yet.
- Guardrail: lead with 1 or 2 proof points per channel, or the candidate becomes the hero.

## Stage 4 · Plan
- Derive 3 steps from how the person actually works, shown across projects. Do not impose a generic process.
- Give each step an audience benefit and evidence.
- Short form: 3 verbs. Avoid jargon-y method names unless the person wants one.
- Keep the deeper internal method for interviews and case studies.

## Stage 5 · Call to action
- Direct: one phrase, used everywhere (for example "Let's talk").
- Transitional: 2 or 3 trust-building offers (flagship case study, AI or skills demo, a free resource). Flag any that do not exist yet. They become the to-do list.
- Avoid: calendar links as the main CTA, "download resume".

## Stage 6 · Avoid failure
- 2 or 3 realistic stakes, each proven by something that happened in the candidate's work.
- One optional sharp line for interviews and posts.
- Never imply that not hiring them guarantees failure.

## Stage 7 · Success
- Outcomes already contributed (with evidence) vs outcomes aspired to at the next company.
- The identity transformation: close the loop with Stage 1.
- A success statement. Often a tagline candidate comes out of this stage.

## Synthesis checklist
BrandScript table · core positioning · value proposition (specific and general) · 3 pillars · voice · proof library · career narrative · tagline decision · clarity audit.
